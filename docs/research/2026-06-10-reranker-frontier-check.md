## TL;DR

- **Aggregation — KEEP MaxP.** 2024-26 work confirms max-over-window-scores is the best *simple, training-free* aggregation and is uniquely *robust as window count grows* (arXiv:2305.18494 — every other simple pooling degrades as you add segments; max stays stable). SumP/AvgP/KMaxP do **not** beat it (CLEF/LongEval explicitly: max > k-max > mean). Only *learned* aggregation (weighted Top-k +1.4 nDCG@10, PARADE-Transformer, kernel pooling) or an evidence-selection pipeline beats it — none free. No change.
- **Model — Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B is defensible but the wrong *shape* for your latency problem.** It's decoder-only (yes/no-logprob scoring) → can't ONNX/quantize cleanly, and it's mid-pack on BEIR (last in its 0.6B class on Jina's harness; stronger on instruction-following in Qwen's own). **Evaluate-first swap: `gte-reranker-modernbert-base` (149M)** — same ModernBERT family as your kept embeddings, encoder (ONNX/CoreML-able), 8192 ctx (kills most windowing), BEIR 56.73 ≈ Qwen at ¼ the params, LoCo long-doc 90.68. Quality alt: `jina-reranker-v3` (in-class BEIR leader, but listwise + non-commercial license + Qwen-sized).
- **Pool size — pool=100 is likely TOO DEEP, not just slow.** "Drowning in Documents" (arXiv:2411.11767): for small cross-encoders, reranking deep *degrades* Recall@10 in ~50% of cases, often below retrieval-alone, especially past ~100. With your strong dense first stage, top-25-50 should match/beat 100. Likely a **4× latency cut with neutral-or-positive quality** — measure pool ∈ {25,50,100}.
- **Speedups — the two biggest are boring: rerank fewer things + use an ONNX-able encoder.** Then window-pruning (pre-select top-2-3 windows/doc by dense score before cross-encoding) is *quality-positive* (EviRerank), and a longer-context encoder removes windowing outright. ONNX INT8 ≈2× CPU at ~100% parity — but only for encoder models, **another strike against the Qwen decoder.** CoreML/ANE = test-don't-assume.
- **Name verification (no hallucinated variants).** Real: Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B, jina-reranker-v3, mxbai-rerank-base/large-v2, bge-reranker-v2-m3, gte-reranker-modernbert-base, Ettin rerankers. `mxbai-rerank-v3-listwise` **IS real** (2026-05-08) but listwise + undisclosed size → not yet a confirmed local option. `bge-reranker-v2.5` **does not exist** (you likely mean v2-m3). *Triage note: all four questions answered in full; nothing dropped.*

## Q1 — Aggregation: is MaxP-over-windows still best simple choice?

**Verdict: YES — keep MaxP. As of mid-2026, max-over-windows is still the best SIMPLE, training-free score aggregation for long-doc cross-encoders, and it is uniquely *robust as the window count grows* — which is precisely your ≤12-windows/doc regime. Everything that beats it requires learning or a heavier pipeline.**

- **[A] "Adapting Learned Sparse Retrieval for Long Documents" (arXiv:2305.18494).** The single most decision-relevant result. Tested {max, sum, mean} × {representation, score} aggregation on MSMARCO-Doc + Robust04. Finding: *"most approaches, except for the max score aggregation, are fragile with long documents; their performance drops severely as more document segments are added. Max score aggregation avoids this downward trend with mostly stable performance over different numbers of segments."* The proximity-matching implicit in max is what keeps it stable. This directly endorses MaxP for "split into many windows, take the max."
- **[A] PARADE (Li et al., arXiv:2008.09093 / TOIS 2024).** Among aggregations that add *no new weights*: PARADE-Max > PARADE-Sum > PARADE-Avg (Robust04; mixed on GOV2). Learned representation-aggregators (PARADE-CNN, PARADE-Transformer) beat all score-pooling — but require training a new aggregation head. Confirms MaxP as the best *free* pooling.
- **[A/B] CLEF/LongEval monoT5 study (CEUR Vol-3740, paper-219).** Grid-searched k-max-avg (k=2..20, best k=4) against max and mean for monoT5 passage scores. Result: max passage aggregation beat mean on all datasets, and *"max passage aggregation was found to be superior to k-max passage, contrary to our hypothesis."* Also notes the choice "only marginally impacts overall effectiveness" — i.e. aggregation is a small lever, and max is the safe end of it. **This kills the KMaxP-beats-MaxP hypothesis for the simple case.**
- **[A] "Efficient Long-Document Reranking via Block-Level Embeddings & Top-k Interaction" (arXiv:2501.17039, Feb 2026).** The one result where something beats MaxP cheaply-ish: on TREC DL'19, AvgP 0.553 → MaxP 0.669 → **Weighted Top-k 0.683** nDCG@10. *"Relying on a single best-matching block is less robust than softly weighting multiple high-saliency blocks."* So a *weighted* top-k can add ~+1.4 nDCG@10 over pure max — but it is a learned/tuned aggregation, not a free swap. Flag as a possible future micro-optimization, not a reason to drop MaxP now.
- **[A] EviRerank (arXiv:2411.06254).** Explicit evidence *selection + composition* before reranking beats MaxP/AvgP pooling-only baselines by a large margin (0.743 vs 0.643-0.654 nDCG@10) AND at lower latency. Relevant less as an aggregation swap and more as support for **window pruning (Q4)**: choosing which windows to score beats scoring-all-and-pooling under tight compute budgets.
- **[A] Set-Encoder (arXiv:2404.06912).** Reassurance that your *pointwise* design is not leaving quality on the table: *"passage interactions do not necessarily improve a cross-encoder's re-ranking effectiveness. A pointwise model … is as effective as state-of-the-art LLM-based cross-encoders in typical Cranfield-style settings."* Listwise only wins when inter-doc aspects (novelty/dedup) matter. So you do not need to switch to a listwise reranker (jina-v3, mxbai-v3-listwise) for standard relevance.
- **Do-not-conflate note:** Several "MAX pooling is worse than AVG/SUM" results (e.g. OpenReview JPjj4GClBr) are about *token→sentence embedding* pooling, a different operation from *passage-score* aggregation. They do not bear on MaxP-over-window-scores.

**What would change this recommendation:** (a) you start fine-tuning an aggregation head — then a learned weighted-Top-k or PARADE-Transformer is worth ~1-2 nDCG@10; (b) you move to a single long-context pass (e.g. gte-reranker-modernbert-base @ 8192, see Q2) that removes windowing entirely, making the aggregation question moot for most docs; (c) your corpus develops strong lead-bias (news-like), where FirstP can rival MaxP — not your case.

## Q3 — Pool-size / rerank depth evidence

**Headline: the frontier evidence says pool=100 is likely TOO DEEP and may be actively hurting, not just slow.** Reducing to ~25-50 is supported on both quality AND latency grounds — but it is model- and first-stage-dependent, so measure on your own eval.

- **[A] "Drowning in Documents: Consequences of Scaling Reranker Inference" (Jacob et al., arXiv:2411.11767; OpenReview LvOaAqOQJY, 2024-25).** The strongest single result here. For modern cross-encoders, scaling K (number of docs reranked) past a small value *degrades* Recall@10. In 53.3% (academic) / 44.4% (enterprise) of experiments, scaling K drove Recall@10 *below retrieval-alone*. "As we increase k further beyond ~100, the rerankers' Recall@10 dramatically decreases" — present in both proprietary and open-source rerankers. Directly implies the current pool=100 sits at/near the danger zone. Their suggested path forward: LLM listwise reranking degrades more gracefully (not relevant to a small local cross-encoder).
- **[C, rigorous] Elastic Search Labs, "Selecting optimal reranking depth" (2024-12).** BEIR-based, nDCG@10 vs rerank depth curves. Finding: on average **90% of the maximum nDCG@10 gain is captured by reranking only ~1/3 of the candidates** (their setup: ~top-100 with a BM25 first stage). Crucial nuance: *the better the first-stage retriever, the fewer candidates you need*; with a strong dense retriever (your gte-modernbert) the recall is concentrated shallow, so the optimal depth is smaller than with BM25. Also: "more effective rerankers are significantly less likely to misbehave after a certain depth" — i.e. weaker/smaller rerankers are MORE prone to the degradation above.
- **[C] ZeroEntropy, "Cascade rerankers" (zeroentropy.dev).** Production rule of thumb: "the downstream cross-encoder typically extracts most of its accuracy from the top-50; beyond that, returns diminish." Common shapes: Dense→200→cross-encoder→10 (cheapest stack); Hybrid→500→cross-encoder→30. Method to tune: measure recall@k of stage-1, pick smallest k where recall plateaus.
- **[A] "Rerank Before You Reason" (arXiv:2601.14224, Jan 2026).** For *listwise LLM* rerankers in deep-search agents (not cross-encoders): d=10→20 buys 5-7 NDCG@5 points; d=20→50 shows clear diminishing returns under medium/high reasoning. Corroborates the "most gain by ~20-50" shape across a different reranker family.
- **[A] Zamani et al., "Stochastic Retrieval-Conditioned Reranking" (CIIR/Google).** Theoretical: challenges the "first stage should optimize recall@N" dogma; for a cascaded system the first stage should maximize *precision* (minimize non-relevant docs passed on), because junk in the pool hurts the reranker. Reinforces "smaller, cleaner pool" over "deep pool."

**Implication for emb:** With a strong dense first stage and a *small* (0.6B) reranker — exactly the regime where the Drowning paper shows degradation — `pool = max(top_k*5, 100)` is the wrong default direction. For `top_k=5`, pool=100 means 4× the windows/compute of pool=25, with literature suggesting the deeper pool is at best neutral and at worst lower recall. **What would change this:** a measurement on your own eval (recall@5 / nDCG@10 at pool ∈ {25,50,100,200}) showing monotonic gains to 100 — if your retriever's recall@5 only saturates at depth ~80, keep a larger pool. The cheap experiment is high-value because the downside (latency) and a plausible upside (quality) both point the same way.

## Q4 — Cheap speedups for windowed cross-encoder rerank

Ranked by ROI for your ~4-5 min/query CPU situation. The two biggest wins are *not* exotic — they're "rerank fewer things" (Q3) and "use an ONNX-able encoder model" (Q2).

1. **Cut the pool (see Q3) — biggest free win, possibly quality-positive.** pool 100→25 is ~4× fewer windows scored. Evidence (Drowning in Documents) says with a small reranker the deeper pool may also *lower* recall. Do this first; it's a config change.
2. **Switch to an encoder reranker + ONNX INT8 dynamic quantization — ~2× CPU, ~100% ranking parity.** sentence-transformers/Optimum report ~2.0-2.1× CPU latency cut from dynamic INT8 with full accuracy retention on STS/ranking; "dynamic quantization usually keeps ranking stable for cross-encoders" (Grade B/C: ST docs, philschmid 2.09×, multiple 2026 walkthroughs). **Critical caveat:** this path is clean for *encoder* cross-encoders (gte-modernbert, bge, MiniLM) but **not for Qwen3-Reranker** — its decoder-only yes/no-token-logprob scoring needs special prompt formatting and does not drop into the standard ONNX SequenceClassification quantize flow (Grade C: ralph-hero M5-Pro research notes, 2026-04-26). So Q2's model swap and this speedup are the same decision.
3. **Window pruning (pre-select windows with the cheap signal) — quality-positive, not just faster.** Your idea — pre-score windows with the dense/BM25 signal and only cross-encode the top few per doc — is well-supported: EviRerank (arXiv:2411.06254) shows explicit evidence *selection* before reranking beats score-all-then-pool both on quality (0.743 vs ~0.65 nDCG@10) and latency under tight budgets; the block-selection lineage (KeyB/IDCM/ICLI, cited in arXiv:2501.17039) reports the same. Concretely: keep top-2-3 windows/doc by embedding-similarity to the query, cross-encode only those, MaxP over them. With ≤12 windows/doc today, top-3 is a ~3-4× window cut on long docs with little expected quality loss (often a gain).
4. **Longer-context model = fewer windows by construction.** gte-reranker-modernbert-base @ 8192 tokens covers ~6,000+ words in one pass; for most of your docs that is 1 window instead of up to 12. This compounds with (1) and removes the aggregation step for short/medium docs entirely.
5. **Dynamic batching across windows.** You already score windows independently → batch *all* windows of *all* pooled docs into uniform-length minibatches (pad to bucketed lengths, sort-by-length to minimize pad). On CPU this mostly helps throughput, not single-pair latency; bigger effect on MPS/GPU. Low effort, do it, but it won't rescue interactive latency alone.
6. **Early-exit cascade (telescoping).** Two-tier: a tiny reranker (ms-marco-MiniLM-L6, 22M, ~12 ms/pair) or the dense score prunes pool→top-k, then the stronger reranker scores only top-k. This is the classic cascade (ZeroEntropy "cascade rerankers"). For your interactive consumer, a cheap MiniLM pre-rank → small final reranker on top-15 is a viable sub-second path. More moving parts; adopt only if (1)+(2)+(3) don't get you under budget.
7. **CoreML / Apple Neural Engine — promising but verify per-model.** Apple coremltools: W8A8 INT8 gets a fast int8 path on the ANE for M4+, palettization (1-8 bit) is best on ANE for memory+latency, INT4-per-block best on Mac GPU (Grade B: Apple docs). BUT cross-encoder transformers don't always map cleanly to ANE, and gains are "highly recommended to test" per-model — Apple is explicit that the compiler may just decompress to float16 and give you nothing. Treat CoreML as an experiment after ONNX, not a guaranteed win. **Caveat for the global rule:** the machine is mid-torch-eval; defer any local conversion/benchmark of these until that frees up.

**What would change the ranking:** if your eval shows pool can't drop below ~80 without losing recall@5, then (2)+(3)+(4) become the load-bearing wins instead of (1). If you must stay on Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B (instruction-awareness proves essential), (2) largely falls away and you lean on (1)+(3)+(5)+MPS.
